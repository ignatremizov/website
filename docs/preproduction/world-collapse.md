# Event Horizon Expedition Reset

## Purpose

The Event Horizon is a secret prestige-reset ritual. It turns deletion of the local
expedition into one final playable scene while preserving a dependable ordinary reset
path for recovery and accessibility.

The full sequence runs only in the active zone. It never loads the other worlds just
to destroy them. Each zone supplies its own reactions to one shared collapse runtime.

## Discovery and arming

The hidden command is:

```text
sudo collapse world --event-horizon --arm
```

The terminal accepts the complete command at any time. The four travel thresholds
teach its body without becoming a hard gate:

| Fragment | Zone | Revealed text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 02 | Luminous Falls | `collapse` |
| 03 | Signal Grid | `world` |
| 04 | Space Archive | `--event-horizon` |
| 05 | Mightymatica | `--arm` |

Field Station portals present the milestone state of their destination. Return
thresholds mirror the world being left, so its progress remains visible from both
sides of the journey. Standard portals grow one low-poly colored crystal around the
stone ring for every recorded catalog milestone. Dormant seeds retain a smaller,
dimmed version of their eventual colour instead of disappearing into the ring. The
Mightymatica cube uses the same count, with its crystals hovering in independent lazy
orbits instead of attaching to a circular frame.

When every catalog milestone represented by a threshold is recorded:

- all of its growth crystals reach full size;
- the portal receives a complete light circuit.

The lantern inscription belongs to the represented world and appears when that world
is complete. Both faces of a threshold therefore reveal the same fragment: Falls
shows `collapse` in Field and Falls, Grid shows `world` in Field and Grid, Archive
shows `--event-horizon` in Field and Archive, and the reconstructed cube shows
`--arm` in Grid and Mightymatica.

From Field Station, the Falls, Grid, and Archive portals therefore act as visible
progress records. Mightymatica remains nested under Grid and reveals its fragment
around both reconstructed cube faces.

When every catalog milestone is recorded, the hub-level `sudo` fragment is revealed
through a terminal congratulations message and the Settings expedition record. The
record presents the assembled command as a button; selecting it opens the terminal
and fills the input without executing it. Only pressing Enter arms the satchel and
stores the knowledge as world meta state. Milestone completion never arms the
protocol automatically.

The canonical command is verb-first. The terminal also accepts `sudo world collapse
--event-horizon --arm` as a forgiving discovery-order alias. Arming does not erase
data. The Settings leaf changes from the ordinary record reset into an animated Event
Horizon control with the same two-step confirmation.
An ordinary reset remains available through the unarmed control and the documented
terminal reset command.

Opening the folio or another input-owning overlay pauses portal dwell. The two-step
confirmation therefore cannot transport a player who opened the satchel while
standing inside a threshold. If another threshold transition is already in flight,
the Event Horizon refuses to start until it has settled rather than allowing the
destination to replace the active zone during the cinematic.

## Reset safety and persistence

Progress is deleted only after the collapse reaches full blackout.
The pre-blackout Last Ember toast is likewise presentation-only. Its durable
world-meta record is written only after the sequence resolves, so interrupting the
final seconds cannot award the hidden milestone early.

The wipe includes:

- expedition achievements;
- indexed and visited routes;
- spell and challenge progress;
- terminal command and current-session world-message history.

Appearance and accessibility preferences remain. Event Horizon knowledge and its
single meta-achievement also remain, because they describe the player outside one
expedition.

Immediately before reload, the shell writes a short-lived session bootstrap marker.
The next page load skips the landing screen and enters the existing
`Opening the Field Station` loading panel.
Ordinary and quiet record resets do not write this marker; they return to the landing
screen as before.

If the tab closes, rendering fails, or the sequence is interrupted before blackout,
the expedition record is not erased.

## Sequence

Normal-motion target duration: 14 seconds.

| Time | Phase | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0–1.6 s | Satchel seed | The folio closes. A black seed leaves the physical satchel and drifts toward the active zone's center. |
| 1.2–3.2 s | Accretion | A black sphere, GPU-driven colored disk, corona, and sparse inward sparks become readable. |
| 2.8–7.8 s | Playable instability | Loose props and small scenery enter eccentric orbits. The player can still move, sprint, jump, cast, and interfere. |
| 5.0 s | Collision release | Prop blockers and elevated support surfaces release as the first terrain seams lift; movement remains available until the final pull. |
| 4.4–10.8 s | Structural collapse | The platform swaps to staggered pentagonal foundation/surface chunks. Individual sections lift, fold, tumble, and spiral inward while large scenery and portals fail around them. |
| 9.0–12.8 s | Final pull | Player control remains visible but loses authority as gravity increases. The camera widens, then follows the singularity. |
| 12.2–14.0 s | Last ember | The hidden milestone appears, the accretion disk flares white and amber, and the world closes to black. |
| after 14.0 s | Reboot | Meta-state is committed, expedition data is cleared, and the page reloads directly into the standard loading panel. |

Reduced motion uses a 2.4-second satchel implosion, static accretion ring, soft flare,
and blackout. It omits orbiting scenery, rapid camera motion, debris, and forced player
movement.

## Zone reactions

### Field Station

- Pencils, compass, planning props, lanterns, and portal crystals leave first.
- Floating cosmic-fabric scraps keep their current tears, folds, stretched edges,
  and pencil repairs while twisting into the accretion stream. Scraps dragged onto
  the platform are pulled with the pieces still floating beyond its edge.
- Outer rock clusters launch upward and outward into faster unstable orbits before
  the final inward pull.
- The tent and atlas resist as larger structural pieces.
- The void turtle remains late in the sequence, flapping hard against the pull while
  its stones form a failing counter-orbit.

### Luminous Falls

- Waterfall sheets lean and then stream upward toward the disk.
- Fish scatter toward the player before the current catches them.
- Rune crystals flash in moon, tide, ember order.
- One local speaker recognizes the Hand closing over the world.

### Signal Grid

- Server lights stutter in expanding outages.
- Pong paddles and the packet rotate around the singularity.
- Hanoi coins lift independently from their pegs.
- The Daemon Controller treats the reset as an unauthorized final protocol.

### Space Archive

- Planets and asteroids enter increasingly eccentric paths.
- Monitor bodies become early debris. The yellow data pane is replaced by
  quality-bounded translucent triangular shards, which burst out of its frame and
  tumble independently before joining the inward stream.
- The heliograph loses optical continuity as instability begins. Collector shafts,
  routed phase ribbons, feedback columns, and the decoded sky shear into
  short colored light fragments. Those fragments flicker, drift with the separating
  hardware, and extinguish before the final pull.
- The observability ring stretches into an oval before collapsing.
- The star atlas remains bright and silver for a short resistance beat.

### Mightymatica

- Solids, needles, chocolate pieces, and carpet tiles lift as recovered experiments.
- Pythagoras receives one final dry line.
- Yuri's source portrait remains coherent and closes into a point of light rather
  than breaking into debris.

## Hidden meta-achievement

**THE LAST EMBER**

> You asked the world to forget you. Something remembered.

This record is absent from the ordinary catalog until earned. Its timestamp and the
zone where the collapse was witnessed live in persistent world meta-state, outside
the expedition wipe.

## Runtime boundaries

`WorldCollapseSequence` owns:

- the singularity, disk, corona, flare light, particles, and instanced debris;
- snapshots of active-zone top-level transforms;
- deterministic orbit, contraction, scale, and opacity envelopes;
- phase timing and one-shot cue flags;
- normalized progress and shared instability/camera/pull envelopes consumed by
  scene-level integrations;
- quality and reduced-motion behavior.

`PortfolioGame` owns:

- starting the sequence and resolving its completion promise;
- retaining player movement during the playable phases;
- releasing stale collision and support surfaces at the sequence cue;
- camera framing and final player pull;
- portal milestone displays and zone-specific actor reactions;
- speech and achievement messages.

While collapse is active, the shell admits only messages explicitly assigned to the
Event Horizon narration channel. Incidental splash, socket, proximity, exhibit, and
physics observations remain suppressed so the active zone's farewell stays readable
until the Last Ember cue.

`AmbientGrid` remains a scene-level system rather than active-zone scenery. It owns
the finale transforms for its visible scraps so the collapse moves their existing
cloth state instead of replacing it with undeformed participant proxies. Its orbit,
pull, and shrink thresholds consume normalized sequence progress rather than
duplicating the cinematic's duration.

The shell owns:

- the destructive confirmation;
- hidden-command arming and world meta persistence;
- clearing expedition stores after successful completion;
- the direct-to-loading reload marker;
- accessible status and reduced-motion presentation.

## Performance budget

The collapse runtime lives inside the already-lazy game bundle and remains inactive
until launched. Arming only persists protocol state; opening a terminal from the
running world requires no second cinematic fetch. It does not create one physics body
per scene triangle.

| Tier | Accretion particles | Instanced debris | Active transform participants | Target |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Low | 36 | 24 | 28 nearest/most meaningful roots | stable 30 FPS |
| Medium | 84 | 52 | 56 roots | stable 45–60 FPS |
| High | 168 | 96 | 96 roots | stable 60 FPS on desktop GPU |

Additional constraints:

- one shader draw for the accretion disk;
- one draw each for the black core, corona, particles, and debris;
- at most two instanced terrain-fracture draws, using 3×3, 4×4, or 5×5 source
  sampling by quality and excluding cells beyond circular platform bounds;
- one Archive-only instanced glass-shard draw using 3×2, 4×3, or 5×4 cells split
  into paired triangles;
- portal milestone crystals use two instanced draws per threshold;
- static scenery moves as existing batched roots or deterministic visual proxies;
- ambient cloth uses its existing shared dynamic line buffer and allocates only one
  small transform record per visible scrap when the finale begins;
- no framebuffer lensing pass in the first implementation;
- large-world deformation begins after collision release;
- quality changes made before arming select counts once and do not allocate per frame.

## Acceptance

- The reset cannot erase progress before blackout.
- The complete hidden command works without collected fragments.
- Portal growth exactly matches recorded catalog milestones for its destination.
- Completed portals reveal the correct world-owned numbered fragment on both sides,
  only under lantern light.
- Completing the catalog reveals `sudo` in the hub without arming the protocol.
- The expedition-record command button fills the terminal; only Enter arms it.
- The reconstructed Math cube uses orbiting milestone crystals.
- The collapse begins wherever the player currently stands.
- Terrain breaks into independently rotating chunks rather than preserving and
  shrinking its original disk or rectangle silhouette.
- Field rocks visibly launch before converging, and Archive glass breaks into
  translucent shards rather than leaving as one pane.
- Movement, sprinting, and jumping remain usable during playable instability.
- Portal travel and ordinary interactions cannot interrupt an active collapse.
- Low and reduced-motion modes preserve the story beat without high particle or
  transform counts.
- Reload after collapse shows the standard loading panel without flashing the landing
  page.
